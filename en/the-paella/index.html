The Paella

Paella: much more than a rice dish

Paella comes from Valencia and takes its name from the wide, shallow pan in which it is cooked. That shape is essential: the rice lies in a relatively thin layer, so liquid can evaporate and every grain absorbs as much flavour from the stock as possible.

But paella isn't only about technique. In Spain it is the ultimate food for sharing. The pan takes centre stage and family or friends gather around it. To me, that social side is at least as important as the recipe itself.

Rice, fire, stock and patience

A good paella starts with the right proportions. The diameter of the pan, the amount of rice, the liquid and the strength of the fire all have to match. And the heat you need changes during cooking: frying, building the stock, cooking the rice and finally resting each ask for something different.

The ingredients matter too. Where possible, I choose authentic Spanish ingredients and fresh produce. But in the end, paella isn't the sum of expensive ingredients. Above all, it is the art of making rice, stock, heat and timing work well together.
